The day Huang returned: a 31% rebound in Nvidia and what it did to every market

November 23, 2026

NoteWritten two sessions later

This entry was written on November 25, after the next two sessions were known. It analyses Monday, November 23 in hindsight, carries no scored prediction, and is excluded from the scored record.

Observation

The news

  • Nvidia announced that its board had voted unanimously to reinstate Jensen Huang as president and chief executive, effective immediately. He had resigned on November 13.
  • The stock had lost nearly a third of its value since November 8.
  • Stocks were already rising before the announcement, after last week’s selloff left the market oversold.

Mechanism

The chain

  • Risk premium, down. The selloff of November 20 came from doubt about Nvidia’s leadership and accounts. Reinstating the chief executive removed the leadership part of that doubt, so investors demanded less extra return to hold the stock.
  • Flows, reversed. The market was oversold. Short sellers had to buy back, and buyers who had stepped aside returned, which turned a repricing into a 31% day.
  • AI and space equities, up most. They had fallen most and are the most sensitive to changes in risk appetite. They are the two most volatile groups I track.
  • Yields: short end up, long end steady. With the crisis easing, the Federal Reserve is freer to raise rates in December, so short-term yields rose. The 10-year barely moved.
  • Dollar, up. Higher expected US short-term rates raised the return on dollars.
  • Gold, up. This is the mirror image of November 20. Gold fell that day because investors sold it to cover losses. When the forced selling stopped, gold rebounded. It supports the flows explanation in my November 20 entry.
  • Oil, down 4.5%. It fell on the same day, and it is worth keeping separate: reports put it down to supply, not to the Nvidia news. Two things moving on the same day are not necessarily connected.

Points to study further

  • Gold and the dollar rose together, which is unusual. Gold was recovering from forced selling while the dollar was responding to rate expectations: two different causes on one day.
  • The 10-year yield barely moved on a day of very good news, and it fell two days later on a day of hot inflation. In this market the direction of long-term yields seems to follow demand for safety more than the inflation data.

What happened next

  • Tuesday, November 24: Nvidia fell 5.9% to $155.38, semiconductors averaged -6.0%, and the space group -7.4%. QQQ lost 2.0% while defensives and healthcare rose, and the Dow closed above 50,000 for the first time. See the entry of November 24.
  • Wednesday, November 25: hot inflation data, new tariff proposals, and more selling in AI stocks. See the entry of November 25.
  • Over the three sessions: Nvidia kept an 18.9% gain from the November 20 close, SMH 5.6%, and SPY 3.9%.

Lesson

One piece of company news can remove one risk. It cannot answer a different question. The return of the chief executive settled who runs Nvidia. It did not settle whether AI spending will earn a return, what the investigations will find, or how high rates will go. A relief rally is as large as the fear it removes, and it fades when the remaining questions come back into view.

For my own method: check the cause of each move separately before connecting them.

Quant research and trading topics

Each topic starts from something the market did that my framework did not fully expect. A research topic turns that surprise into a rule that can be tested on past data before any money is involved. The trading uses are conditional: they describe how the result would be used if the test supports it, and none is a recommendation.

1. How much of a relief rally is kept

  • Question: After a single-day gain above 10% in a stock, what share of the gain remains after 5 and 20 trading days?
  • Why it may matter: A jump can come from new information, which tends to last, or from short sellers buying back, which tends to fade. Nvidia gave back 5.9 points of its 31.5 the next day.
  • How I can test it in this journal: Extend the price download to five years. Find every day with a move above 10% in the watchlist, and measure each stock’s return relative to its group over the following 5 and 20 days.
  • Trading use, if it holds: The result says whether to follow a large move or wait for it to settle.

2. Sympathy moves beyond normal sensitivity

  • Question: When the sector leader jumps, do peers that rise more than their usual link to the leader implies give back the excess?
  • Why it may matter: On a day like this, buyers lift the whole group without distinguishing between companies. The space group rose 8.8% on news about a chipmaker.
  • How I can test it in this journal: Estimate each semiconductor and space ticker’s sensitivity to Nvidia over the previous 60 days. On the shock day, compute the excess move (actual minus expected) and check whether it reverses over the next three days.
  • Trading use, if it holds: A pair trade between names that over-reacted and names that under-reacted.

3. Gold as a gauge of forced selling

  • Question: Is gold falling together with stocks a sign that investors are being forced to sell, and does its recovery mark the end of that selling?
  • Why it may matter: Gold fell with stocks on November 20 and rebounded with them on November 23. In ordinary stress the two move in opposite directions.
  • How I can test it in this journal: Compute a rolling five-day correlation between GLD and SPY. Mark the days when both fell more than 2%, and study SPY over the following five days.
  • Trading use, if it holds: A flag for capitulation: evidence that a selloff is driven by forced sellers, not by new information.
